Understand legal language

From dense clause to clear meaning.

JURISAI doesn’t just summarise. It takes a clause apart — the terms of art, the plain meaning, and what it actually asks of you.

1 · Original clauseEmployment_Contract.pdf · §11.2 · p.4
Either party may terminate this Agreement upon thirty (30) days’ written notice, provided that the Company may, at its sole discretion, elect to pay salary in lieu of notice, and the Employee shall remain bound by clause 9 following termination.
3 terms of art identified
2Legal terminology
Sole discretion
The company decides alone, and doesn’t need your agreement or a reason.
In lieu of notice
Paying you instead of having you work the notice period.
Remain bound
Some duties survive after the job ends — here, those in clause 9.
3Plain-language explanation

Either you or the company can end the job by giving 30 days’ written notice. The company can choose to pay you for those 30 days and let you go immediately instead. Whichever way it ends, certain promises you made in clause 9 still apply.

4What it means for you
  • Notice must be in writing — a conversation alone may not count.
  • The company can end your work early, but must still pay for the period.
  • Check clause 9: it may limit where you can work next.
5Questions you may want to ask
Detect contradictions

Two documents. One conflict no one noticed.

Contracts, policies and notices are written at different times by different people. JURISAI reads them together and surfaces where they disagree — with both sources side by side.

SOURCE A

Notice period30 days written notice by either party

Company Policy

Notice period60 days notice before termination of employment

JURISAI finding

Potential contradiction detected

Review required
Source A

Employment Contract §11.2 requires 30 days’ notice.

Source B

Company Policy §4.3 requires 60 days’ notice.

Why it matters

The notice period you owe — or are owed — may differ by 30 days, affecting pay, start dates and obligations during the gap.

What JURISAI can’t decide

Which document takes precedence may depend on an order-of-precedence clause or applicable law. This is flagged for a qualified person to review.
